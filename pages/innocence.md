## Prisons and Innocence Narratives

Prison literature can take several shapes, ranging from firsthand incarceration accounts to journalistic work to scholarship to carceral fiction. The innocence narrative is yet another category, often overlapping with the aforementioned forms. Like other kinds of prison literature, the tradition illustrates the flaws of the justice system, ranging from dangerous prison conditions to unfair legal processes. However, innocence narratives have exceptional power. They help us learn how stories are distorted, what makes someone “believable,” and the ways that money, power, and structural inequality can obscure the truth. These works also offer insight into how people grapple with wrongful incarceration, both while imprisoned and once they are released. 

This exhibit analyzes innocence narratives across time, tracing these stories from the early Middle Ages until today. Each innocence narrative has unique context and characters, yet they all have meaningful implications for the genre of prison literature and our understanding of the justice system. Weaving these stories together helps us hold the system accountable and identify opportunities for change — an essential step toward reforming the carceral state.
